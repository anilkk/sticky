# The positioning interview

Ask before drafting. Ask three to five questions per round, wait, then follow up. The goal is to
collect the author's facts and their customers' exact words, not the author's summary of them.

## Round 1: the customer

```txt
Who is the customer who gets the most value fastest, and would complain loudest if you shut down?
  Role, company type, team size, and the moment they went looking for a fix.
What were they doing right before they found you? The trigger, not the persona.
Who else is in the deal, and what does each of them care about?
```

Push once if the answer is a segment ("mid-market SaaS"). Ask for one real account.

## Round 2: the alternative

```txt
If your product vanished tomorrow, what would that customer do instead?
  A competitor, an open-source tool, a spreadsheet, a hire, or nothing.
What do they lose by going back to it? Use their words if you have them.
Who do you lose deals to most often, and why do you lose?
```

"Nothing" and "build it ourselves" are valid alternatives and often the main ones.

## Round 3: the difference and the proof

```txt
What can you do that those alternatives cannot? List attributes, not adjectives.
For each attribute: what does it let the customer do, and how do you know?
What numbers, customer names, benchmarks, or demos can you publish today?
What would a skeptical buyer say is not true about your claim?
```

Record what is publishable separately from what is internal-only.

## Round 4: the customer's words

```txt
Paste three to five things customers have said, exactly as they said them.
  Sales calls, support tickets, reviews, Slack, churn interviews.
When a happy customer explains you to a colleague, what do they say?
Which phrase do your reps use that always gets a nod?
```

These lines are source material. Quote them exactly, with who said them.

## Round 5: the market opinion

```txt
What changed in the market that makes the old way stop working?
What do you believe that most vendors in your category would disagree with?
Where is your buyer on awareness: do they know they have the problem, or are they comparing vendors?
```

## Gaps

When an answer is missing, do not fill it. Leave a precise marker in the doc:

```txt
[TK: which alternative do you lose to most? One name is enough.]
[TK: is the 40% time saving from a customer, a benchmark, or an estimate?]
```
