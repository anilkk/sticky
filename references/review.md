# Review format

Review what the user supplied. Do not draft a replacement or change files unless asked. Lead
with the largest material issue; line edits come second.

## Piece-level verdict

Omit fields that would add ceremony rather than information.

```txt
Surface:      what this is and where the reader meets it
Customer:     who it is written for, or "not identifiable"
Alternative:  what it is implicitly compared with, or "none named"
Difference:   the lead differentiated claim, and whether it passes the logo-swap test
Proof:        which claims are supported, attributed, or unsupported
Awareness:    the stage it assumes, and whether that matches the surface
Retell:       the one sentence a buyer would likely repeat after reading
Top fixes:    the two or three changes with the largest expected gain, ranked
```

If Customer, Alternative, or Difference is missing, say so first and offer the interview. Line
edits cannot supply positioning.

## Line-level finding

One block per material issue:

```txt
Line:         the shortest quote that locates the issue
Verdict:      keep / revise / ask-customer / cut
Pattern:      a name from edit.md
Why:          what the line does instead of its job
Suggestion:   a supported replacement, a precise question, or the reason to cut
Safety check: whether the suggestion keeps facts, attribution, conditions, and legal text
```

Verdicts:

- `keep`: a pattern is present but earned, for example a category term the buyer searches for.
  Say what earns it.
- `revise`: the source already contains what an honest improvement needs.
- `ask-customer`: the fix needs evidence only the author or their customers have: a quote, a
  number, a named alternative. Ask exactly for it and offer a plain fallback.
- `cut`: the line adds only repetition, unsupported emphasis, or ceremony.

## Discipline

- Prefer three high-impact findings to a long word watchlist.
- Report no finding when the copy already does its job.
- Do not fabricate a sample replacement that contains invented proof.
