# What each surface owes the reader

| Surface | Reader's stage | Must carry | Can drop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage hero | Solution- or product-aware, five seconds | What it is, for whom, the main outcome, one action | The market opinion, feature lists |
| One-liner / elevator pitch | Any; spoken aloud | Customer, alternative, difference in one breath | Proof details (have them ready) |
| Messaging doc | Internal | Value prop, three benefits, features as proof, stakeholder translations, story, TKs | Polish |
| Launch post | Problem-aware | The shift, what was wrong before, what changes, proof, how to try it | Exhaustive features |
| Sales deck | Problem- to product-aware | Insight, alternatives, value themes each with proof, objections | Company history |
| Battlecard | Internal, mid-deal | Where we win, where we lose, traps to set, proof, landmines | Brand voice |
| Product or use-case page | Product-aware | Capability, use case, outcome, proof, next step | The origin story |
| Email or ad | Usually problem-unaware | The problem in the reader's words, one claim, one action | Everything else |

## Developer audiences

- Show what it does before what it means. Capability plus outcome beats an abstract value prop.
- Proof is code, a benchmark with method, pricing, limits, and docs. Logos matter less.
- Keep precise technical terms. "SDK", "rate limit", and "p95 latency" are not jargon here.
- The call to action is usually an install command, a free tier, or a quickstart, not "book a demo".

## Regulated or legal claims

Security, compliance, health, and financial claims keep their exact wording and conditions. Flag
rather than rewrite them, and do not shorten a disclaimer.
