# Messaging diagnoses

Diagnose in this order. Fix the highest-ranked problem before touching words.

## 1. No customer

The copy addresses "teams", "businesses", or "everyone". Fix: name the best-fit customer by role
and trigger, or ask for them. Do not guess a persona.

## 2. No alternative

The copy explains the product without saying what it replaces. The reader cannot judge the
value because there is nothing to compare against. Fix: name the alternative (competitor type,
DIY, manual process, doing nothing), or `[TK]`.

## 3. No difference (the logo-swap test)

Replace the product name with a competitor's. If the line still reads as true, it is
category-level, not differentiated. Fix: lead with the unique attribute and the value it
creates. Keep category-level lines only where the reader needs to know what the product is.

## 4. Unproven claim

Superlatives and outcomes with nothing beside them: "fastest", "most reliable", "10x",
"trusted by leading companies". Fix: attach proof from the source, soften to an attributed
claim ("customers report"), or cut. Never invent the proof.

## 5. Wrong awareness level

A problem-unaware reader gets a feature comparison, or a vendor-comparing reader gets a lecture
on why the problem matters. Fix: match the surface to the stage in `surfaces.md`.

## 6. Feature soup

A list of features with no benefit on top. Fix: group features under the benefit they prove;
drop features that prove nothing the buyer cares about.

## 7. Jargon

Words the buyer would not say out loud. Test each word: does the buyer use it, and does it mean
something specific to them? Precise technical terms the buyer uses daily are not jargon.

Common offenders: leverage, seamless, robust, unlock, empower, revolutionize, cutting-edge,
next-generation, best-in-class, world-class, end-to-end, holistic, synergy, game-changing,
supercharge, streamline, frictionless, turnkey, innovative, solution (as a noun for product).

## 8. We-speak

The copy talks about the company ("We are a leading provider...") instead of the reader. Fix:
make the customer the subject. Keep "we" where a commitment is being made ("we refund...").

## 9. Padding

Throat-clearing intros, stacked adjectives, restated benefits, and generic closers ("and so
much more"). Fix: cut, then check that nothing the reader needs went with it.

## Fidelity safeguards

- Keep every number, name, condition, and disclaimer exactly as given, or flag it.
- "Up to", "in our tests", "on average", and "for most customers" are conditions. Keep them.
- Do not upgrade "used by" to "trusted by", or "helps" to "guarantees".
- Do not merge two customers' quotes or reattribute a quote.
- Check your own rewrite for the same patterns you flagged in the source.
